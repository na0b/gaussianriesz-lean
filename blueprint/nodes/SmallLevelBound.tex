\begin{lemma}[The small-level estimate, with constant one]
Let $G : \mathbb{R}^n \to \mathbb{R}^n$ be any measurable vector-valued function
and let $0 < t \leq M$.  Then
\[
  t\,\gamma_n\bigl(\{x : |G(x)| > t\}\bigr) \;\leq\; M ,
\]
where $|\cdot|$ is the Euclidean norm on $\mathbb{R}^n$.  The constant is $1$,
better than the target constant $2$, and the estimate requires no property of $G$
beyond measurability.  The borderline level $t = M$ is included, so no
decomposition at a borderline height is needed there.
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The measure $\gamma_n$ is a probability measure by \noderef{GaussIsProb}, so every
measurable set has $\gamma_n$-measure at most $\gamma_n(\mathbb{R}^n) = 1$.  In
particular
\[
  \gamma_n\bigl(\{x : |G(x)| > t\}\bigr) \;\leq\; 1 .
\]
Multiplying by $t > 0$ preserves the inequality, giving
\[
  t\,\gamma_n\bigl(\{|G|>t\}\bigr) \;\leq\; t .
\]
Finally $t \leq M$ by hypothesis, so the left-hand side is at most $M$.
\end{proof}
