\begin{lemma}[Subordination identity for the square root]
For every real $\lambda \geq 0$ the integral below converges absolutely and
\[
  \frac{1}{2\sqrt{\pi}} \int_0^\infty \bigl(1 - e^{-s\lambda}\bigr)
      \, \frac{ds}{s^{3/2}}
    = \sqrt{\lambda} .
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
The integrand is nonnegative, since $1 - e^{-s\lambda} \geq 0$ for
$s, \lambda \geq 0$, so absolute convergence is the same as finiteness of the
integral, which the computation below establishes.

If $\lambda = 0$ the integrand vanishes identically and both sides are $0$.

Assume $\lambda > 0$.  Near $s = 0$ we have $1 - e^{-s\lambda} \leq s\lambda$, so
the integrand is at most $\lambda s^{-1/2}$, which is integrable on $(0,1]$; near
infinity the integrand is at most $s^{-3/2}$, which is integrable on
$[1,\infty)$.  Hence the integral is finite.

Integrate by parts on $[\varepsilon, R] \subset (0,\infty)$ using
$\frac{d}{ds}\bigl(-2 s^{-1/2}\bigr) = s^{-3/2}$ and
$\frac{d}{ds}\bigl(1-e^{-s\lambda}\bigr) = \lambda e^{-s\lambda}$:
\[
  \int_\varepsilon^R \bigl(1-e^{-s\lambda}\bigr)\frac{ds}{s^{3/2}}
   = \Bigl[-2s^{-1/2}\bigl(1-e^{-s\lambda}\bigr)\Bigr]_\varepsilon^R
     + 2\lambda\int_\varepsilon^R e^{-s\lambda}\, s^{-1/2}\, ds .
\]
As $\varepsilon \downarrow 0$ the boundary term at $\varepsilon$ tends to $0$,
because $1 - e^{-\varepsilon\lambda} \leq \varepsilon\lambda$ gives
$2\varepsilon^{-1/2}(1-e^{-\varepsilon\lambda}) \leq 2\lambda\varepsilon^{1/2} \to 0$;
as $R \to \infty$ the boundary term at $R$ tends to $0$ because
$0 \leq 1 - e^{-R\lambda} \leq 1$ and $R^{-1/2}\to0$.  Therefore
\[
  \int_0^\infty \bigl(1-e^{-s\lambda}\bigr)\frac{ds}{s^{3/2}}
    = 2\lambda \int_0^\infty e^{-s\lambda} s^{-1/2}\, ds .
\]
In the last integral substitute $s = u/\lambda$, so $ds = du/\lambda$ and
$s^{-1/2} = \lambda^{1/2}u^{-1/2}$:
\[
  \int_0^\infty e^{-s\lambda}s^{-1/2}ds
    = \lambda^{-1/2}\int_0^\infty e^{-u}u^{-1/2}\,du
    = \lambda^{-1/2}\,\Gamma(1/2)
    = \lambda^{-1/2}\sqrt{\pi}.
\]
Combining,
$\int_0^\infty(1-e^{-s\lambda})s^{-3/2}ds = 2\lambda\cdot\lambda^{-1/2}\sqrt\pi
= 2\sqrt\pi\,\sqrt\lambda$, and dividing by $2\sqrt\pi$ gives the assertion.
\end{proof}
