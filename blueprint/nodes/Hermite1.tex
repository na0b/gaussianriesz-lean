\begin{definition}[The normalized one-dimensional Hermite functions]
Let $\mathrm{He}_k \in \mathbb{Z}[X]$ be the probabilists' Hermite polynomials,
determined by
\[
  \mathrm{He}_0 = 1,
  \qquad
  \mathrm{He}_{k+1} = X\,\mathrm{He}_k - \mathrm{He}_k'
  \quad (k \geq 0).
\]
For $k \in \mathbb{N}_0$ define $h_k : \mathbb{R} \to \mathbb{R}$ by
\[
  h_k(x) = \frac{\mathrm{He}_k(\sqrt{2}\,x)}{\sqrt{k!}} .
\]
This rescaling is dictated by the variance $1/2$ of the one-dimensional measure
$\pi^{-1/2} e^{-x^2}\,dx$: the substitution $y = \sqrt2\,x$ carries that measure to
the standard Gaussian, against which $\mathrm{He}_k$ is orthogonal with
$\int \mathrm{He}_k^2 \, d\mathcal{N}(0,1) = k!$.  Equivalently, $h_k$ is the classical
positive-leading-coefficient Hermite polynomial $H_k$ for the weight $e^{-x^2}$
divided by $(2^k k!)^{1/2}$, which is the normalization used in the reference
paper; the two powers of $2$ in $H_k(x) = 2^{k/2}\,\mathrm{He}_k(\sqrt2\,x)$ and in
$(2^k k!)^{1/2}$ cancel exactly.
\end{definition}
