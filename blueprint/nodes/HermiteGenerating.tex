\begin{lemma}[Generating function of the probabilists' Hermite polynomials]
Let $\mathrm{He}_k$ be the probabilists' Hermite polynomials of
\noderef{HermiteDerivative}.  For all real $y$ and $z$ the series below converges
absolutely and
\[
  \sum_{k=0}^\infty \mathrm{He}_k(y)\,\frac{z^k}{k!}
    = e^{\,yz - z^2/2} .
\]
\end{lemma}
\begin{proof}
Fix $y \in \mathbb{R}$ and set $G(z) = e^{yz - z^2/2}$, an entire function of
$z \in \mathbb{C}$ restricted here to real $z$.  Let
$c_k = G^{(k)}(0)$ be its Taylor coefficients at $z = 0$, so that
\[
  G(z) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty c_k \frac{z^k}{k!}
\]
with absolute convergence for every $z$, because $G$ is entire (the exponential
of a polynomial) and hence equal to its everywhere-absolutely-convergent Taylor
series.  It therefore suffices to prove $c_k = \mathrm{He}_k(y)$ for every $k$.

Step 1 (a recurrence for the $c_k$).  Differentiating $G$ gives
$G'(z) = (y - z)\,G(z)$.  Writing both sides as power series,
\[
  G'(z) = \sum_{k=0}^\infty c_{k+1}\frac{z^k}{k!},
  \qquad
  (y-z)G(z)
    = \sum_{k=0}^\infty y\,c_k \frac{z^k}{k!}
      - \sum_{k=0}^\infty c_k \frac{z^{k+1}}{k!}
    = \sum_{k=0}^\infty \bigl(y\,c_k - k\,c_{k-1}\bigr)\frac{z^k}{k!},
\]
where in the last step the second sum was reindexed by $k \mapsto k+1$ and
$\frac{1}{k!}\cdot k = \frac{1}{(k-1)!}$ was used, with the convention that the
term with $k = 0$ is absent (equivalently $c_{-1}$ is multiplied by $0$).  Two
everywhere-convergent power series are equal only if their coefficients agree,
so
\[
  c_{k+1} = y\,c_k - k\,c_{k-1}, \qquad k \geq 0,
\]
with the $k=0$ instance reading $c_1 = y\,c_0$.

Step 2 (the same recurrence for $\mathrm{He}_k(y)$).  By the defining recurrence
of the probabilists' Hermite polynomials, which is shared context,
$\mathrm{He}_{k+1} = X\,\mathrm{He}_k - \mathrm{He}_k'$; and by
\noderef{HermiteDerivative}, applied at index $k-1$,
$\mathrm{He}_k' = k\,\mathrm{He}_{k-1}$ for $k \geq 1$, while
$\mathrm{He}_0' = 0$ because $\mathrm{He}_0$ is the constant polynomial $1$.  Hence,
evaluating at $y$,
\[
  \mathrm{He}_{k+1}(y) = y\,\mathrm{He}_k(y) - k\,\mathrm{He}_{k-1}(y), \qquad k \geq 0,
\]
with the same convention at $k = 0$, where it reads
$\mathrm{He}_1(y) = y\,\mathrm{He}_0(y)$.

Step 3 (matching initial data and concluding).  We have $c_0 = G(0) = 1$ and
$\mathrm{He}_0(y) = 1$.  Both families therefore satisfy the identical two-term
recurrence of Steps 1--2 with the identical value at $k = 0$; note the recurrence
determines $c_{k+1}$ from $c_k$ and $c_{k-1}$ alone, and at $k=0$ determines
$c_1$ from $c_0$ alone.  By induction on $k$, $c_k = \mathrm{He}_k(y)$ for every
$k \geq 0$.  Substituting into the Taylor expansion of $G$ gives the asserted
identity, with the absolute convergence inherited from that expansion.
\end{proof}
